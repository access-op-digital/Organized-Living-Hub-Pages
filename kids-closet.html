Kids’ Closet Shelving and Components

Hanging Rods for Short and Long Garments

Use the length of the clothes on their hangers to set the space below each rod. Double hanging sections suit shorter items; a separate long-hang section leaves room for dresses, robes or longer outfits. freedomRail Clothes Rods sit below compatible shelves using Rod Clips and the correct shelf brackets. The 14-inch-deep freedomRail Wood Shelves accommodate clothes rods beneath them.

Shelves for Folded Clothes and Shoes

Give pajamas, T-shirts and sweaters separate shelf spaces so a child can find a category without moving a tall mixed stack. Set aside a lower shelf for paired shoes, checking the actual shoes against its depth. freedomRail Wood Shelves use 5/8-inch thermally fused laminate; the 48-inch shelf comes in 8-, 12- and 14-inch depths. Match each shelf to its specified brackets.

Drawers for Socks and Small Clothing

The freedomRail OBox 3 Drawer Unit has two shallow drawers and one deeper drawer for separating smaller items. It mounts directly to freedomRail Uprights and measures 24 inches wide, 16 inches high and 14 inches deep. Use the compartments for socks, underwear and accessories, with the most-used drawer positioned for the person who will open it.

Baskets and Bins With Clear Categories

Open bins can make returning clothing and a small collection of toys more straightforward. Label them with words, pictures or both, and keep each category simple. For a mounted pull-out option, the Reveal Canvas Basket has full-extension glides and fits freedomRail Uprights spaced 24 inches apart. Its removable canvas liner allows spot cleaning. Measure its extension path before deciding where it belongs.

Kids’ Closet Layouts for Growing Wardrobes

Small Reach-In Closet

In a reach-in, place the daily hanging section and the most-used drawers where the door opening exposes them. A narrow shelf section can separate folded clothing from hanging outfits. Check sliding or bifold doors before choosing a pull-out basket: an accessory needs room to extend through the opening, even when the closet is wide enough behind it.

Nursery Closet That Can Change Later

Begin with the caregiver’s routine: current-size outfits together, folded clothing in its own section and spare sizes clearly labeled. Where space permits, three levels of short hanging space use the closet height while garments are small. A freedomRail layout can later lose a hanging level or gain shelf space as garments get longer and the child begins choosing outfits independently.

Closet for Independent Dressing

Place the child’s most-used clothes within comfortable reach. Keep clothing categories recognizable, such as school outfits, everyday tops and pajamas. Check that the child can take an item out and return it to the same place. Adjust that section as reach and clothing sizes change.

Shared Kids’ Closet

Give each child an identifiable hanging section and shelf or drawer space. Use names, pictures or different label colors to show which belongings go where. Adjust each section to that child’s clothing lengths and reach, and use a separate labeled space for clothing being saved for the other child.

Walk-In Closet for Longer Clothes and More Categories

A walk-in can divide hanging clothes, folded items and shoes across more than one wall. Keep the route into the closet clear when drawers and baskets open, and allow longer garments to hang freely. As the wardrobe changes, review whether a former toy or bin section would be more useful for clothing, footwear or accessories.

Kids’ Closet Size and Reach Guide

Measure the closet and the belongings before choosing a layout. A child’s age alone does not determine a useful rod height or shelf position. Check what the child can comfortably retrieve and return, and separate daily storage from items an adult manages.

How We Specify and Test Kids’ Closet Shelving

Organized Living conducts internal engineering and quality testing at its Bloomington, Indiana manufacturing facility, covering load capacity, durability, performance and product quality. Use the specifications and installation instructions for the selected product, including its support spacing and hardware. Frequently Asked Questions

What belongs in a kids’ closet?

Start with clothing and shoes, then add defined space for accessories, puzzles, small building sets or books. Give daily outfits priority. If toys need most of the room, consider a separate playroom storage plan so the closet can still support getting dressed.

Can the closet change as a child grows?

Yes. Compatible freedomRail shelves, hanging sections and accessories reposition after installation. Review the layout when clothing lengths, shoe sizes or reach change. Built-in and fixed systems have different adjustment limits, so confirm those before selecting a line.

How high should a child’s clothes rod be?

Use the child’s comfortable reach and the length of the garments to plan the daily rod position. Have them try taking an item out and returning it. A separate section can hold clothes managed by an adult. Recheck the arrangement over time instead of relying on one age-based height.

Should I use open shelves, drawers or baskets?

Match the storage to what goes inside and how it will be put away. Open shelves make folded categories visible, drawers separate smaller clothing, and open bins group loose items. A mounted pull-out basket also needs the correct Upright spacing and enough room to open through the closet doorway.

How can outgrown clothes be kept out of daily storage?

Sort clothing at seasonal changes and remove pieces that no longer fit from the everyday section. Label items being saved by size and keep them separate from clothes currently in use. Decide which pieces to pass on before adding more storage for them.

Can drawers be added to an existing kids’ closet?

Yes, if the existing system accepts the selected drawer unit. An OBox 3 Drawer Unit mounts to compatible freedomRail Uprights. Identify the existing system and check its mounting layout before ordering. For another product line, ask a Dealer or our support team which drawer options fit that system.

How much does a kids’ closet system cost?

The price depends on the system, the amount of shelving and hanging space, the selected finish and accessories, and any installation work. Build the design and review its component total in the shop, or request a Dealer quote for the complete project.

When will a kids’ closet order arrive?

Most products leave our warehouse within 3–5 business days. Arrival also depends on transit time, the items ordered and the destination. Use the order’s tracking information once it ships to follow the delivery.
